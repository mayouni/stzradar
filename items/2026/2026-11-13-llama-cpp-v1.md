## What happened

The team behind llama.cpp, now part of Hugging Face, released version 1.0.0 of llama.cpp and of ggml, the tensor
library under it, on November 13, 2026. The project is three and a half years old and its team counts more than 150
model architecture variants in its source. Until this week both libraries changed their C API whenever the work asked
for it, and applications followed build numbers. From 1.0.0 both follow semantic versioning: a breaking change to the
public C API in the `include` folder raises the major version, a backward-compatible feature, backend update, model or
API addition raises the minor version, and a bug fix raises the patch version.

The release clears the ground for that promise. `llama.h` removes 35 deprecated functions and takes in the API for
speculative decoding; ggml removes its deprecated functions and the legacy `DIAG_MASK` operations, and its RPC protocol
now refuses a peer of an incompatible version. New in the same release: key-value caches stored in 8-bit floating
point, an unbuffered model load on Windows, a host-memory cache for the experts of mixture-of-experts models, three
text architectures and one speech model. `llama-server` closes several network holes (SSRF through its proxy and media
URLs, CORS origins, DNS rebinding), sends its Hugging Face token only to huggingface.co hosts, and cancels a tool call
when its client disconnects. The team names NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm, Arm, IBM and Intel as the providers of the hardware
its tests run on.

## Why it matters to Softanza

Softanza runs its models on the user's machine, and its engine builds its own decoder, embedder, OCR and vision graphs
directly on ggml, compiled into the engine from a vendored copy. That copy is ggml 0.29.0, taken on November 12 from
the upstream release of November 4, pruned to the CPU backend and carrying a few patches of Softanza's own. Each move
of that copy has so far meant reading upstream's history between two tags to learn what changed.

Version 1.0.0 changes what a version number tells Softanza. Inside the 1.x line the functions the engine calls keep
their shape, so a later 1.x brings its speed work and new formats at the price of a rebuild and the checks; a 2.0.0
announces the day the engine's calls need a review. The distance from 0.29.0 is nine days of upstream work, and the
engine calls none of the functions 0.29.0 already marked as deprecated, so the step is small now and grows with every
week of waiting.

The release also confirms the ground Softanza chose: models on the user's own device, in the GGUF format, on a library
that six hardware makers test on their own machines.
