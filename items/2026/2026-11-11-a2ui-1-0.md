## What happened

The A2UI project, begun at Google, released A2UI 1.0 on November 11, 2026, the first stable version of its open
protocol for generative user interfaces, under the Apache 2.0 licence. An agent answers with a screen instead of a
wall of text: a stream of JSON messages creates a surface, lists its components, fills its data model and later
removes it. The components come only from a catalog the app registered (a card, a chart, a choice picker, a button),
and the app's renderer draws them with its own native widgets, in React, Angular, Lit, Flutter and SwiftUI, with
Jetpack Compose announced. The project states its safety principle plainly: the agent sends data, never executable
code, and a schema bounds what it can ask for.

Version 1.0 adds typed function calls in both directions, so a picker can ask the agent whether a slot is free while
the user is choosing; components from several catalogs on one screen; a whole screen in a single message; and styling
left entirely to the app's own theme. Beside the JSON form, A2UI Express describes the same screen in about three
times fewer output tokens, by its authors' measure, and the format can also carry the output of decision models.
Agent SDKs exist for Python, TypeScript and Dart. Gemini, Atlassian and ByteDance ship it, and the project names
Alation and Bloomberg among its early builders.

## Why it matters to Softanza

Zane, Softanza's language for user interfaces, works one level above A2UI. A Zane declaration says what the human is
doing, in 22 closed verbs, and its compiler checks it against a constitution of 123 rules before anything is
rendered; its own README puts it as widgets being consequences of intent, never primitives. A2UI's catalog is exactly
that set of widgets, and its renderers are exactly the native reach Zane's projections aim for. The two fit as layers:
a declaration Zane has checked can be projected into A2UI messages, and from there every A2UI renderer draws it.

The other direction matters as much. Agents across the industry will now send screens, and the protocol locks which
components they may use but says nothing about whether the screen is any good: a verbless button, twelve sections
where four would do, a choice with no way back. Zane's constitution is written to refuse precisely those, and an A2UI
stream is data it can read and judge before it reaches a user.

For Softanza's own agents, which run small local models, the size of a screen in tokens is a cost paid on every
answer. A2UI Express claims a threefold saving over JSON; a Zane declaration is compact by design, and the engine's
constrained decoding can hold a small model to either grammar. Which one a 1B model writes validly, and at what length,
is a measurement to make before choosing.

## What we do

- Propose to Zane an A2UI v1.0 projection, and a check of incoming A2UI streams against the constitution.
- Propose to the intelligence plane a measurement of Zane, A2UI JSON and A2UI Express as a small model's output.
- Look again by 2027-01-11.

  A2UI Express once in 100 tokens; Zane's declarations are the shortest lawful screens and covered fewer of the
