## What happened

open486, a project of Vayner Systems written up by Lev Vayner, rebuilds a 486 PC inside one FPGA. Work began on
November 12, 2026 and the site went up on November 22.

- **The board.** A Digilent Arty A7-100T, with an Artix-7 XC7A100T at 50 MHz; the design uses about 59,000 of its
  63,400 LUTs (93%).
- **The machine.** Its own staged, microcoded CPU core for the 486 instruction set with an x87 floating-point unit;
  its own SVGA, Sound Blaster 16, OPL3 and MPU-401 audio, NE2000 network and IDE on microSD; its own BIOS and VGA BIOS.
  Some chipset logic comes from ao486 under its BSD licence.
- **What runs.** DOS and Windows 95 OSR2.5, which gets an address by DHCP and browses the web; DOOM, Quake,
  Wolfenstein 3D and Prince of Persia.
- **The measures.** 174 on TopBench in CrystalMark Retro 2.1.0, at 640x480 in 16-bit colour with 128 MB. A table of
  cycles per instruction from simulation, beside the figures in Intel's 486 manual: a 32-bit `imul` in 5.4 cycles
  against 13 to 42, a 32-bit `div` in 73 against 40.

The source is to be published under Apache 2.0. The project states its figures are early and will change.

## Why it matters to Softanza

Harobanda Edge lowers a declared machine to one firmware for a small device. open486 is a far larger machine built
the same way, from its own parts, and it reports its progress well: what runs, a benchmark with its settings, and a
cycle count per instruction against the original manual. That is a form of evidence worth copying for any machine a
desk builds, and the project is worth watching until its source is published.
