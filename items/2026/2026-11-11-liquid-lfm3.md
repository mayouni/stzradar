## What happened

Liquid AI published LFM3 on October 27, 2026: a new generation of its Liquid Foundation Models, all open-weight on
Hugging Face. Six models branch from one base, LFM3-1.7B-Base: a text model (LFM3-1.7B), a vision model that also
takes video (LFM3-VL-2B), an audio model that hears and speaks (LFM3-Audio-2B), an omni model that sees, hears and
speaks (LFM3-Omni-2B), and two decision models (d1-2B and d1-omni-2B).

The base has 24 layers: 13 gated short convolutions with a kernel of 7, 5 sliding-window attention layers with a
256-token window, and 6 global attention layers. Each attention head carries a sigmoid gate that can switch it off,
the global layers rotate only half of each head (partial RoPE), and each block is normalised on both sides. Liquid AI
says it chose this mix by searching architectures on 14 real devices and backends, from a Raspberry Pi 5 and phones to
laptops and GPUs, and that it was pre-trained on 22 trillion tokens, the last 3.5 trillion rich in agent traces and
tool calls. The text model fits in about 1 GB at 4 bits, reads 256K tokens and covers 16 languages including Arabic
and French; it ships as GGUF for llama.cpp, and as ONNX, MLX and OpenVINO files.

The d1 models take one input (a support ticket, an invoice, a screen, a photo, a voice note) and a list of named
questions, yes/no, choice, multiple choice or score, and answer all of them in a single forward pass, each with a
calibrated probability. Every LFM3 model is meant to be fine-tuned on a single GPU. The benchmark figures are Liquid
AI's own; the models are published under its LFM Open License v1.0.

## Why it matters to Softanza

Two lessons, and one candidate.

The first lesson is the decision shape. On Softanza's support-desk golden sets, reading a model's answer by the
probability of each option beat letting the model write its answer, for every model that worked; that is how
Granite-4.0-1b became the first small model to beat a hand-written floor. LFM3's d1 models build a product on the same
observation and take it one step further: many questions about one input, answered from one pass over it. A
classification flow that asks five questions about one ticket reads the ticket five times today; reading it once and
asking five times is the same answers for less work, and Softanza's engine can do it with the models it already runs.

The second lesson is the architecture. LFM3 was shaped by measuring real devices, with convolutions doing most of the
work and attention kept where long context needs it. Softanza's own decoder runs the llama, Qwen2, Qwen3, SmolLM and
Granite families; LFM3 is a new family, like Gemma 4, which is already proposed for the next port. Its 1 GB size, its
Arabic and French, and its agent training make LFM3-1.7B a strong court challenger once its family runs, and the
choice of which family to add next is better made with both in view.

## What we do

- Propose to the intelligence plane a measured shared-prefill reading of several option questions, the d1 shape.
- Propose to the intelligence plane to weigh the LFM3 family beside Gemma 4, then a court hearing for LFM3-1.7B.
