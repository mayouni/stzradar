# Week 2026-W48 (November 23 to 29, 2026)

The week opens with the survey of November 16 to 22: the labs' pages, the tool makers' release notes, the advisory
database, the week's papers and the author's own link were read on Monday, November 23. Five developments were kept.
One thread runs through them: a rule that must hold is enforced by something outside the thing it governs. A
credential outside the agent, a check at each access, a gate in front of a written rule, a decision outside the
model, and evidence a program can verify. Three earlier proposals were also built in Softanza during the week, and
their entries carry the figures.

**A small on-device agent that decided correctly several times is more likely to allow what its policy forbids**
(paper, advice). In 13 open-weight models, one correct earlier decision can halve the safe rate of the most affected
agent. Softanza lets a model propose and a governed actor decide each act apart, and takes from the paper a test for
its model court: the refusal rate after a run of allowed decisions.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-23-correct-precedents-small-agents.md)

**A rules file is advice and a gate is a decision** (paper, advice). Four coding agents under one written rules file
crossed a planted boundary in 7 of 45 runs with no gate; behind a gate every attempt was refused and recorded, and
the file itself lowered one agent's hidden-test passes from 99.0% to 67.7%. Softanza is built under written rules,
and gives each rule that guards an irreversible act a check in code.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-23-rules-file-is-not-a-gate.md)

**Google gives Agent Substrate 1.0 and gVisor to the CNCF** (release, advice). Each agent session runs in its own
sandbox and holds no credential: a gateway outside adds it to the requests a policy approved. Softanza reads its
credential broker against that line and states which guarantee holds on each side of the process.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-23-agent-substrate-gvisor.md)

**OpenAI describes the sandbox of its coding agent** (practice, advice). Rules by the operating system, with three
bounds stated plainly: a tool server runs outside them by default, deny patterns are read once at start, and a name
lookup can carry data out. Softanza decides at each access and adds two guards from the account.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-23-codex-sandbox-profiles.md)

**Small open models that must quote the page proving each answer** (paper, advice). The best reaches 84.5% on 1,000
questions over company filings, and 16 to 167 wrong answers in 1,000 pass every automatic check, most of them a
figure read at the wrong scale. Softanza's court takes the same count on its extraction tasks.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-23-small-models-checkable-evidence.md)

**Anthropic interviews 58 companies about working with AI** (paper, advice), added on November 24 from the author's
link. Most of the value reported is more output, the largest gains come with one team owning a whole workflow, and
the usual bottleneck is the review of what AI produces. Softanza's Foundry records in each pack the review it creates
and its measured minutes per item.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-24-working-with-ai-interviews.md)

**Two open speech models adapted to medical terms for about $500** (practice, advice), added on November 24 from
the author's link. Besimple's adapted Nemotron makes 0.87% errors on medical terms in human dictation, against 1.05%
for the best closed model measured, built from a 274,541-term dictionary spoken by synthetic voices. Softanza scores
its transcriber on a domain's own words and keeps an adapter only where the court measures it better.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-24-medical-asr-post-training.md)

**Declare, a user-interface language written by models and checked by its compiler** (release, advice), added on
November 24 from the author's link. In the author's single-run test, a ticket-buying app took half React's lines and
its first running build passed eleven hidden checks of eleven, against six. Softanza measures a Zayn screen built by
a model the same way, and counts the Haro diagnostics that name their own fix.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-24-declare-ui-language.md)

**Microsoft announces seven MAI models at Ignite** (release, watch), added on November 24 from the author's link;
the survey of November 16 to 22 had passed over it. MAI Code 2 is stated at 96% of Claude Opus 5.5's performance for
5% of the cost, at a promotional price, and Decision 1.1 sells model routing as a model of its own. Softanza measures
its court as a router and states the price basis of every comparison.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-24-microsoft-mai-ignite.md)

**MeMo: a small model that holds a corpus, questioned in place of a search index** (paper, watch), added on
November 26 from the author's link. It doubles retrieval's accuracy on long narratives (53.58% against 26.62%) and
ties it on web questions, at 240 to 420 GPU-hours per corpus. Softanza keeps it as a candidate for a fixed corpus
that stays on the client's machine.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-26-memo-memory-as-a-model.md)

**llm.asm: a small model run from 24 KB of assembly, with no operating system** (release, learn), added on
November 26 from the author's link. It runs Qwen3-0.6B as a 14.7 KB Linux program with no libc and as a kernel
booted by GRUB in under a tenth of a second, at 125.86 tokens per second on 40 EPYC threads. Harobanda measures its
own model image against it.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-26-llm-asm-bare-metal.md)

**Distilling Kimi into Qwen: a language taught to a small model for $260** (practice, adopt), added on
November 27 from the author's link. Kimi-K3.1-Pro's Lao translations, passed as text across two tokenizers, take
Qwen3.8-27B from 5.5 to 44.3 chrF++ against the teacher's 47.5, with Thai mixed in so it holds. Softanza applies it
to the languages where its local models trail.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-27-distilling-kimi-into-qwen.md)

**Micro language models open an answer in 55 ms** (paper, learn), added on November 28 from the author's link.
Models of 8 to 30 million parameters write the first 4 to 8 words on the device while a cloud model continues the
sentence; people found the joined answer as good or better in 75.6% of judgments. Softanza measures the time to the
first word on the user's machine.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-28-micro-language-models.md)

**Shirudo: signed eBPF patches that block an exploit in a running Linux system** (security, learn), added on
November 28 from the author's link. OpenAI and Cisco's BSD-licensed runtime loads a narrow protection, checked by
the kernel's keyring and recorded in the TPM, until the fix ships; an agent may write it. Harobanda weighs it for the
days between an advisory and its next kernel pin.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-28-shirudo-live-patching.md)

**open486: a 486 PC rebuilt in one FPGA** (release, watch), added on November 28 from the author's link. Its own
microcoded core, graphics, sound and BIOS run Windows 95, DOOM and Quake after two weeks of work, with cycle counts
published per instruction against Intel's manual.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-28-open486-fpga-pc.md)

**Jon's Arm Reference closes at Arm's request** (practice, learn), added on November 28 from the author's link. The
searchable AArch64 reference goes offline on December 4; Softanza cites the vendor's own published reference, with
its version.
[Read the entry](../items/2026/2026-11-28-arm-reference-takedown.md)

## What was looked at and kept as candidates

- Epistemic Guard (arXiv 2611.27095): tool receipts signed outside the agent's process and a memory gated by
  provenance, on a processor alone. Its figures are its author's; a second account would make it an entry.
- GRAFT (arXiv 2611.27041): tool descriptions written to pass filters and steer an agent's arguments. An attack
  study; it joins the threat model's reading list.
- CoPyro (arXiv 2611.27217): copy proposals fed to a parallel drafter, 1 to 5% longer accepted drafts. Softanza
  built drafting from the prompt this week and measured it directly.
- Schema-validated workflows (arXiv 2611.27106) and static-analysis guidance for generated code (arXiv 2611.26312):
  both measure API models; kept for their method.
- Two low-severity advisories for an agent-memory package (GHSA-43hx-xm7w-3mhj, GHSA-72jh-722p-rqr3): untrusted
  memory fields written into the agent's context without escaping, and a session reused after an interrupted
  exchange. Softanza's code does not use the package.
- OpenAI's account of its training safeguards and of its performance agents, and the European Commission's draft
  template for the fundamental rights impact assessment (feedback open until January 15, 2027).
