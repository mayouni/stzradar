# Why Lookout exists

Softanza Radar exists so that what changes in AI each week reaches the part of Softanza it concerns, as
work someone can act on, and so that what was learned is kept where every reader finds it again. This page says
what it answers, what the author asked of it, what it grew into, and what it gives Softanza.

## What it answers

AI changes every week. New projects appear, papers change how programs are written and checked, labs ship
features, companies change how they sell and govern, and security researchers find holes and the fixes for them.
Softanza is the governed software platform of the AI age, so almost every one of these touches something it builds:
its library and engine, its language Haro, its declared machine Harobanda, its interfaces, its intelligence layer.

A finding that matters has to reach the part of Softanza it concerns within days, as work someone can act on. The
first week showed why: a coding agent's "don't ask again" writes the exact command, password included, into a file
that projects publish. That reached Softanza's maintainers the day it was read, as a check to run and a rule to adopt.

Lookout also keeps what used to end with each conversation. Reading a paper or an advisory inside a working
session produced an opinion that the next session no longer had. Lookout writes it down once, with its sources, where
every session, every project and every reader can find it again.

## What the author asked for

The author opened Lookout on 2026-11-08 and set its work in five parts:

1. **Read at the source**: projects, papers, lab and product features, commercial practices, security findings and
   fixes, regulation, and the links and ideas the author brings.
2. **Judge each one from Softanza's point of view**: adopt it, learn from it, correct it, defend against it, watch it,
   or decline it with the reason.
3. **Write it once, in two forms in one file**: a page for people and data for programs, so the two never drift apart.
4. **Propose the work it implies** to the Softanza project it concerns, which decides how to do it.
5. **Publish a feed** that any command line, any Softanza tool or application, and one day Softanza's own assistant
   can read and turn into notices for developers, users, learners and maintainers.

And one condition over all of it: the watch is public, so that everyone benefits from what it reads.

## What it grew into

Each requirement that followed came from using it:

- **A signed feed.** A feed that puts text in front of people is something to defend, so every feed file carries an
  SSH signature, checked by this site and by the bar in the reader's own browser.
- **A site anyone can use.** The week on a radar, Softanza layer by layer, every signal filtered, and the trends over
  the weeks, each signal written in the order its reader needs it.
- **A bar for other sites.** One script line puts the newest notice for a site's readers at the top of its pages.
- **Any project can run its own.** One file, `lookout.yaml`, says which project a watch serves and what its agent
  learns, so the same tool serves projects well beyond Softanza.
- **Pages people can read.** One reading size, one column, one scroll bar: the rules of Zane, Softanza's interface
  constitution.

## What it gives Softanza

- **Each project hears what concerns it**, as a dated proposal, and the trends show how fast Softanza answered.
- **A record that lasts**: every entry with its sources, its stance and its corrections, never deleted.
- **The first form of a feature the stack will carry**: an assistant that tells its people that something changed in
  the world, and what it means for them.
